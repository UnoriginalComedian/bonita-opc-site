The idea

Parish bulletin, not tech startup

The reference points are the printed Sunday bulletin and a hymnal: ruled panels, flat tinted grounds, small-caps labels set in a serif, and information density that respects a reader's time. Warmth comes from color, typography, and real photography — never from rounded corners, drop shadows, or gradients.

The palette is drawn from the building and the place itself: Bonita stucco, terracotta roof tile, eucalyptus and chaparral, and the congregation's own orange accent color.

Color

Two accents, two jobs

Tile always means "do this" — a link, a button, a call to action. Brass always means "this is a label or a flourish," never an action. Don't swap them.

Every pairing above already clears WCAG AA for body text. The whole palette flips automatically for dark mode — the site does this with CSS custom properties; reuse the token names, not the raw hex, anywhere the guide gets implemented in code.

Type

Vollkorn + Alegreya Sans

A sturdy book serif paired with a humanist sans drawn for literature, not software UI. The contrast between the two carries the identity — don't set headings in the sans or body copy in the serif.

Signature details

Reuse these, don't reinvent them

Three components that make the site (and anything printed alongside it) recognizable at a glance:

Morning Worship · 11:00 a.m.

"Judgment for Turning Back"

Evening Worship · 6:00 p.m.

"Redemption in Christ"

The order-of-service panel — brass top border, small-caps header, dotted dividers. The most distinctive component on the site; its structure should carry over to the printed bulletin.

A ruled panel, not a card — flat --stock background, a hairline border, no shadow, no radius beyond 2px. This is what should show up on a printed handout too.

The double rule — borrowed straight from a hymnal's section dividers. Good for a printed program or a slide transition; don't use a single rule where this is the intent.

Voice

Plain and literal

Say

  • "Communion, followed by the Feast Day fellowship meal."
  • "There is a lot in front of the building, with accessible spaces close to the doors."
  • Scripture as the lead — a Psalm, not a slogan.

Not

  • "Join us for a special time of fellowship!"
  • "Experience worship like never before."
  • Unexplained churchy jargon aimed at people who already know it.
Do & don't

Keep it a bulletin, not a product

Do

  • One accent color per action — tile for "do this," brass for labels.
  • Real stroke-style SVG icons, matching the site's menu/sun/moon marks.
  • Asymmetric layouts where one element deliberately outweighs another.
  • Real congregation photography whenever it exists.

Don't

  • Drop shadows, glassmorphism, or radius beyond 2px.
  • A third accent color.
  • Emoji as icons, anywhere.
  • Center everything by default.
